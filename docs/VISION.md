# Why EduSphere exists

Read this before touching anything. It explains what the product is for, so that a
change made years from now still serves the same purpose.

## The problem

School takes six hours a day and most of that is not learning. It is waiting, moving
between rooms, and sitting through material a child already knows or is not ready for.
The children who fall behind are rarely stupid. They were moved on before something
stuck, and everything built on top of it wobbled.

## The bet

Two hours of focused, adaptive practice a day can cover the academic ground, and cover
it better, because nobody is pushed forward until the thing before is solid. The other
four hours become the point of childhood: reading for pleasure, cooking, building,
being outside, being bored, learning to speak to people and to handle their own minds.

Alpha School has shown this is possible with an AI tutor and roughly two hours a day.
EduSphere is a version of that idea that a homeschooling family or a microschool can
run on an iPad, with no account, no server, and no child's data leaving the room.

## What the product must always do

- **Master, then move.** A module opens only when everything it needs is mastered.
  Mastery is measured in one sitting, never patched together across days.
- **Go back when needed.** Repeated misses send the student back to the prerequisite,
  even one that looked solid. A gap further back is usually the real problem.
- **Bring it back.** Old material returns inside later rounds. If it did not stick,
  we find out, and the educator sees it.
- **Never invent.** Every question is generated by arithmetic and checked by an
  independent test. No AI writes questions at question time.
- **Say it in words.** Educators read sentences, not tables. Children who cannot read
  get pictures, sound and one thing to tap.
- **Keep nothing we do not need.** A school-issued ID, what was assigned, what
  happened in practice. Nothing a child writes. No names, photos, or trackers.
- **Reflect often, and for a reason.** Reflection questions come round after every
  second finished round, pass or fail, and every one is written toward one of four
  aims: curiosity about the world; failure as the way learning happens; naming and
  handling feelings; and the truth that hard days are a normal part of being human
  and that people get through them. After a failed round, the questions about failure
  and feelings come first. No question is ever offered a third time. The pool must
  grow with the curriculum, and a check says so when it falls behind.
- **Stay neutral.** The reflection questions carry four voices and none is right.
  Nothing argues for or against religion. Nothing reaches a child until the school
  approves it.

## What the product is not

It is not a replacement for a teacher. It is the part of teaching that a machine does
better: patient, endless, correct practice, with an honest record. The teacher does the
rest, and the life skills section exists to give them ideas for it.

It is not a claim. We do not say students outperform anyone until real classrooms have
shown it. The curriculum maps to published state standards, and that map is checked
by a test, and that is the extent of what we promise.

## Who it is for, in order

1. Microschools and homeschooling families, who share the philosophy and can run it on
   a handful of devices with a file for a backup.
2. Families supplementing a traditional school, once the library is wide enough.
3. Daycare centers, with pre-K material that prepares for kindergarten.
4. Trades and workplace training, later, with the reflection and life skills sections
   switched off. Every part of the platform that assumes a child can be removed by one
   setting so this stays possible.

## How to tell if a change is right

Ask: does it help a child master something, or help an educator see clearly what
happened, or free up time for real life? If it does none of those, it does not belong.
If it stores something about a child we did not need, it does not belong. If it lets
a student move past something they have not mastered, it is wrong.
