# Audit, 2026-09-23

A pass through the whole product looking for bugs and rough edges in what exists, with the fixes made in the same pass. No new features.

## What was checked, and how

- Every one of the 422 modules opened at phone width (360px) in a real browser: the lesson, its story and the first practice question, watching for script errors, console errors and any page wider than the screen. Result after fixes: 0 errors, 0 overflows, 0 failures.
- The rule suite (1,501 checks), the story suite (4,283 checks including the new course-story rule), the pictures, ledger, spelling and coverage suites, the browser walk (166 checks) and the tour measured on a laptop and a tablet (every card fits, none scrolls).
- Read-through of the texts a student or educator meets first: welcome, account creation, backup nudges, the Backup page, the tour's ten cards, the report and weekly note, the Story Log page, the transcript, the standards map.

## Bugs found and fixed

1. Grade 1 read-aloud lessons crashed. Ten grade 1 modules (teen numbers, adding and subtracting to 20, tens and ones, comparing to 100, writing numbers, reading a word, sh-ch-th, silent e, reading a sentence, what happened) were written as paragraphs with the words to read inside [[ ]], but the read-aloud lesson screen expected a script or an example and threw on the first one. The screen now builds the script from the paragraphs: the instructions are spoken and the bracketed words are shown on a card, never spoken, which is the standing rule. This one mattered: a first grader could not open ten lessons.
2. The stack drawing broke past five levels: boxes shrank to negative widths and ran off the card (the console showed it on the new Staff lesson). The drawing now scales to its levels, up to eight, and a rule holds every stack to eight.
3. Course stories were showing under Most recent on the Story Log even when unread (fixed last pass; a rule now covers the length and shape of every course story).
4. The memory check now favors the mastered modules with the most recent misses (the one thing from the last list that was a yes), with a rule.

5. Copy: five lines an educator meets early read wrong (a misused i.e. and chrome book in the device-name example, insures for protects on the Backup page, a Quick checks sentence that said allow for by allowing, login as a verb, and for obvious reasons on the transcript). Fixed.
6. A second sweep covered every older-student game and its Instructions, Let's Read and a course story, My progress, coloring pages, the classroom with a student, the report, the per-student story log, the transcript, the standards map, Backup, Wonder Questions, Reading Lists, Experiments and Life Skills at phone width: zero errors, zero overflows.

## Looked at and left alone

- Mastery tracking, spaced memory checks and confidence grading have their rules and passed them; the triple dose keeps the same bar, scaled.
- Backups: the automatic backup on sign-out and on a student's Exit is exercised by the browser walk, and a restore resets the PIN and opens the classroom.
- Remembrance days: September 11, Veterans Day and Memorial Day fire on their dates and stay quiet otherwise (a rule now covers 2026's dates). They appear as a card on the student courses page and the classroom page, once per day, and an educator can hide one for the year.
- Animations respect reduced motion; nothing pulses or orbits for a reader who has asked for stillness.
- The license notice sits at the top of every source file and the built page.

## How long the platform takes, and what a full year needs

Minutes per module, estimated honestly: a lesson read (3, or 5 read aloud), its story (2), a practice round (4, or 3 read aloud), about two and a half rounds to master since mastery needs passes on separate days plus memory checks, and a minute of another-way reading. Games and coloring add about a third of the time for the early years and about a sixth for older students.

About 144 hours in all. A school year is about 180 days. Used as a daily supplement for half an hour, a grade needs about 90 hours; used three times a week for twenty minutes, about 60. Today a grade holds 7 to 17 hours, so a student who works every day finishes their grade in two to five weeks and then waits.

What fills a year, per grade, in order of value:

1. Depth before breadth: two to three more modules for every existing course, so each course teaches the whole strand it names rather than its headline ideas. That alone roughly doubles the hours.
2. The missing core strands, by grade: Math in every grade needs a second course (measurement and data in K to 5; statistics and probability in 6 to 8; a modeling course in 9 to 12). Reading needs a literature course beside the skills course from grade 3 up (novels, poems, drama, with the stories doing the reading). Science needs earth science in 3 to 5 and a lab course in 6 to 8. History needs geography in 3 to 5 and civics in 6 to 8 (it exists only in grade 3 and grade 12).
3. Electives that the early grades lack and the older grades want: Music and Art in every grade (they exist in 1, 3 and 4), Technology in every grade from 3 up (it exists in 3, 5 and 7), Health in every grade (K and 4), a Spanish course from grade 3 (nothing yet), a personal-finance course in 8 and 11 beside the grade 12 one, and a study-skills course in 6 and 9.
4. Longer rounds for older students: eight questions a round from grade 6, ten from grade 9, which lifts time per module without more writing.

Put together, a grade wants roughly ten times its current modules to fill a year at half an hour a day: about 240 modules across eight to ten courses. At the pace of this build (thirty to sixty modules a pass with stories and standards), that is five to eight passes per grade. The order above is the order to do it in, and the holiday remembrances, the course stories about the four characters, and the games matched to topics should keep pace so a year in the app has a shape and a cast, not only a count.
