# EduSphere, in plain English

A learning platform for schools that teaches every student at their own pace, proves what they learned, and gives back the hours a normal school day spends on one-size-fits-all instruction.

## Standout features
- **Mastery, not seat time.** Students advance only after proving they understand. Academic work fits in a fraction of the day, freeing time for life skills a screen can't teach.
- **Efficiency you can measure.** Minutes per day and learning gains are recorded, so schools see real numbers.
- **Confidence score, 1 to 5.** Built from correct answers, streaks, speed against the student's own pace, and later recall. The formula is printed on every report.
- **Learning that sticks.** Mastered skills reappear in later lessons at growing intervals. Slipping shows up before a teacher has to look.
- **Reports that can't be fudged.** Every action is a permanent record, never edited or deleted. Every number is recomputed from it, with its definition shown. Resets start fresh; history is never rewritten.
- **"Wonder" time.** Open questions with no right answer, then four views — a scientist, an artist, a grandparent of faith, a skeptic — none declared right. Goal: curious students who expect to be partly wrong and keep improving. Human-approved, neutral on religion and non-belief; a student's words are never stored.
- **Kindergarten-ready.** Read aloud in a natural voice, answered by tapping pictures and big numbers. One engine, K–12.
- **Teachers pointed to who needs them.** Two failed sets in a row puts a student on the teacher's list, with confidence, recall, and time at a glance.
- **Privacy by design.** Schools only. Stored: a school-issued ID, a class, the learning record. Never: names, emails, photos, voice, free text. IDs created by admins, picked from a list, never typed. No ads or trackers. Built for FERPA, COPPA, and state student-privacy laws.

## How schools use it
- Courses grouped by subject; admins turn subjects on or off per class — works for one classroom or a whole district.
- Admin accounts: reports, material, student accounts (add, rename, merge, reset), struggling-student alerts.
- Automated, customizable reports for a student, class, school, or district.
- Sign-in the school's way: class code + student ID, or Google / Clever / ClassLink. No child passwords held.
- Coverage maps per grade: every standard (Texas TEKS first, Common Core alongside) and the lessons that teach it. Not complete until every standard is covered.
- Accessible by default: dyslexia-friendly type, high contrast, screen readers, captions.

## Adding your own material
- White-glove first: EduSphere builds custom modules from PDFs, slides, spreadsheets, images, video, audio.
- Then tiers: Standard (EduSphere library), Plus (self-upload with AI assistance and admin approval), Enterprise (white-glove onboarding, custom content, district sign-on, scheduled reports).
- Verification pipeline: material is read, split into modules, ordered foundations-first, fact-checked with every claim traced to a source, checked for age fit and enough practice. Nothing may state a fact it can't cite. Loops until clean, asks a person when unsure, and a lead admin approves before students see it. Teachers can propose edits into the same review.
- Beyond school: medicine, law, business, trades, enterprise software.

## Under the hood
- Python + FastAPI, PostgreSQL with row-level security (educators see only their students), React. Automated daily backups.
- Core-library questions are generated by arithmetic and verified by hundreds of automated checks.

## Two ideas set aside
- **Public ledgers (Hedera):** not in the core product — nothing about a child belongs on a permanent public chain, and it doesn't improve backups or uptime. Possible later use: proving a completed credential.
- **"Right-brain" framing:** the Wonder feature is real; the left/right-brain claim is disputed, so we describe the goal — reflection, curiosity, comfort with not knowing yet.
