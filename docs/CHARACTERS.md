# Characters

The core cast of the story-based learning layer. They appear only where the moment is big enough to deserve them: key ideas, turning points, things that changed the world. Most stories belong to someone else (Maya the baker, Silas and his lily) or to no one at all (a duck, a boat, a clock tower). A reader should never feel introduced to a crowd.

## The clock

Characters age faster than the learner, so a student who starts in pre-K meets them as small children and, by high school, as adults with families. Each band is a chapter:

| Band | Chapter | Mike | Chloe | Frederick | Savanah | Georgette |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-K to grade 2 | Children | 3 to 8 | 3 to 8 | 3 to 8, gets the digital microscope at 7 | not yet met | already in her fifties, the godmother figure |
| Grades 3 to 5 | Teens | 12 to 16, working hard, learning to build | 12 to 16, painting everything | 12 to 16, first experiments | 12 to 16 on the farm, met late in the chapter | sixties |
| Grades 6 to 8 | Young adults | 17 to 22, building and fixing for a living | traveling, every kind of art | 20, the lab, the jar of pond water | 17 to 22, leaves the farm for the city | sixties |
| Grades 9 to 12 | Adults | father of Jaxon (6) and Harlow (3) | an artist who has seen the world | the professor with the gray handlebar mustache | powerful and influential in the city, Mike's wife | seventies, wise, well read |
| College | Elders and legacy | fifties | fifties | emeritus | fifties | very old, the keeper of the family's stories |

## The people

- **Mike**, the skeptic. A street-hardened but warm man with a rough-edged childhood, not the worst, not the best. Physical work taught him to build, fix, and keep going, and, the hard way, to reframe failure, handle big feelings, and stop keeping up with anyone. Thinks like a fallibilist: hold ideas loosely, test them. Calisthenics, gymnastics, cycling, music. Bald with a close dark beard as an adult. Frederick taught him the scientific method; they become friends. When science fails him or life gets hard, he goes to Georgette.
- **Chloe**, the artist. Bright, patterned, layered clothes. Every kind of art. Wants to see the world and have new experiences, and does. The one who notices what things look like.
- **Frederick**, the scientist. His dad's digital microscope started it at seven. Cellular and molecular biology, then a professorship. As an old man: a large gray handlebar mustache and always a white lab coat. Introduces experiment and method to Mike.
- **Georgette**, the grandparent of faith. Not related by blood: the godmother of Mike's uncle, and so a fixture of Mike's childhood. Short auburn hair, a red coat, a book-filled study with a round window and a teacup. Well read, cultured, wise. Faith is part of who she is and never a sermon; she gives advice, comfort, and, when asked, prayer.
- **Savanah**, Mike's wife. Started with nothing in a small farm town where she learned the hard skills of life. Wanted more, went to the city, and became powerful and influential there. Copper-red hair, a work shirt on the farm, a smart coat in the city. Their two children come along in the adult chapter.
- **Jaxon**, six, Mike and Savanah's son. Sensitive; cares for everyone in need. Sandy light-brown hair, a green checkered shirt.
- **Harlow**, three, their daughter. Mischievous, adorable, very sharp. Blonde curls in pigtails with sage-green bows.

## Rules

1. Savanah, Jaxon and Harlow appear only in wholesome scenes: no conflict aimed at them, no scenes that invite criticism of how they live, nothing a parent would wince at. Mike, Chloe, Frederick and Georgette can carry harder stories.
2. Georgette is not Mike's grandmother. She is the godmother of his uncle.
3. The core cast gets the most significant modules: the ideas that changed the world. Everyone else gets everyone else.
4. Introduce at most one new core character per story, and never all of them at once. Across a band a student should meet the cast the way you meet neighbors, one at a time.
5. Faith stays respectful and personal to Georgette; no story argues for or against religion.
6. Every scene with a core character names its character sheet (C serial) in docs/ART-REQUESTS.md so they look the same everywhere.
